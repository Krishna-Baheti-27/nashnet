GAME THEORY IN MULTI-AGENT SYSTEMS

Can selfish AI agents share a scarce cloud without wrecking it?

NashNet drops 10 autonomous AI tenants into a city edge cloud with limited GPU, bandwidth and power. Each tenant decides for itself whether to share or cheat. We change the platform’s rules and measure what happens to everyone.

THE PROBLEM IN THREE STEPS
01

A shared cloud

Every AI job needs GPU, bandwidth and power. They come from shared zones spread across the city, and zones only regrow slowly.

02

The temptation to cheat

Sharing makes both sides better off (+2 each). But taking without giving wins any single round (+3 vs −1). That is the Prisoner’s Dilemma.

03

The tragedy of the commons

Hoarders drain zones below their safe reserve. A drained zone regrows slowly, so everyone starves, and tenants start going offline.

RULES THE PLATFORM CAN SWITCH ON
Memory
Tenants remember who cheated them.
Targets: cheating in trades
Reputation ledger
A public trust score everyone can check.
Targets: cheating in trades
Commons quotas
Nobody may take more than 3 units or drain a zone below 40%.
Targets: draining the commons
Congestion pricing
Taking from a nearly empty zone costs extra credits.
Targets: draining the commons
Random audits
40% chance to catch a cheat or over-drain: fine plus repayment.
Targets: both problems
